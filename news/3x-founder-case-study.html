The Pattern: One Founder, Three Companies

Most people are still figuring out their first real job at 18. Jaden Medina has founded three companies: Ocean Development, CampusBite, and Mako. Three different products, three different problems, built by the same person before he could legally rent a car.

Being a 3x founder this young isn't about chasing a title. It's what happens when someone treats building companies as a repeatable skill rather than a one-time stroke of luck.

Ocean Development: The Agency That Started It All

Jaden taught himself web design and development at 13. By 15, that turned into Ocean Development, a Miami-based web design and development agency that has since grown into a small, capable team serving clients across the United States. Ocean Development is where Jaden learned the fundamentals that every founder needs: how to sell, how to deliver, how to build a team, and how to keep client relationships intact when things don't go perfectly.

It's also the company that funded and informed everything that came after it. The discipline of running client work, deadlines, scope, communication, became the operating foundation for the ventures that followed.

CampusBite: Betting on a Second Vertical

CampusBite was Jaden's move from service business to startup. Where Ocean Development is built around client work, CampusBite is a company of its own, built around serving college campuses. Taking on a second company while still running the first meant learning to switch contexts: agency work pays the bills and sharpens execution, while a startup like CampusBite demands a different kind of patience, building something before there's a guaranteed client on the other end.

Mako: The Newest Build

Mako is Jaden's latest company, started after he turned 18 this past January, the clearest sign that this isn't a one-off story. Starting a third company after already running two is a different decision than starting the first one. It means the earlier ventures are stable enough to support new ones, and it means the itch to build didn't go away once there was already a successful agency and a startup to point to.

What Building Three Companies Teaches You That One Doesn't

  • Systems over hustle. Running one company on willpower is possible. Running three requires actual systems, delegation, and people you trust to own pieces of the business without you in the room.
  • Different businesses, different muscles. A services agency, a campus startup, and a product company each demand different skills, sales-led versus product-led, local versus scalable, client-funded versus self-funded.
  • Conviction compounds. Each company made the next one easier to start, not because the work got easier, but because the fear of starting something new gets smaller every time you've already done it.

Why This Matters

Three companies by 18 isn't the point of the story, what each of those companies required to get built is. Ocean Development taught execution. CampusBite taught how to build without a guaranteed outcome. Mako is the latest proof that the pattern holds. Jaden's track record isn't a single win, it's a repeatable approach to starting things and seeing them through.
