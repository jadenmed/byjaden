How a Team Led by an 18-Year-Old Generated $3,000,000 for Client Businesses

Inside the systems, strategy, and small team Jaden Medina built at Ocean Development to help client businesses generate over $3,000,000 in combined revenue.

The Milestone

At 18 years old, Jaden Medina has led a small, remote team at Ocean Development that has helped client businesses generate a combined $3,000,000 in revenue. That number isn't Ocean Development's own revenue, it's the revenue their work has helped produce for the local service businesses, e-commerce brands, and small companies they've designed and built websites for.

It's a milestone most agencies don't hit for years. Jaden's team got there while he was still a teenager, running the business out of Miami with a core team operating remotely across different cities.

Building a Team Before Most People Finish High School

Jaden started teaching himself web design and development at 13. By 15, that skill had turned into Ocean Development, a real agency with real clients. Getting from "one person freelancing" to "a team capable of producing seven-figure results for clients" meant bringing in people who could match the standard he'd set for himself.

That team includes Micah Brown, COO and Lead Developer, operating out of Toronto since day one. Rather than hiring based on proximity, Jaden built a structure that runs on trust and output, letting the team work across cities while holding the same bar on every project.

The Approach: Websites Built to Convert, Not Just Look Good

The $3,000,000 figure didn't come from traffic or impressions, it came from actual sales, bookings, and leads. That's the difference between a website that looks good and one that performs. Ocean Development's process focuses on a few consistent things across client work:

  • Clarity over decoration. Every page is built around one job: getting the visitor to take the next step, whether that's booking a call, making a purchase, or filling out a form.
  • Conversion-first design. Layout, copy, and calls-to-action are structured around how real customers actually make decisions, not just what looks clean in a mockup.
  • Speed and technical execution. A site that's slow or broken on mobile loses customers before the design even matters, so performance is treated as a design requirement, not an afterthought.
  • Ongoing iteration. Many client relationships don't end at launch. Ocean Development continues to adjust and improve sites based on what's actually converting post-launch.

Who This Has Helped

The businesses behind that $3,000,000 are mostly small and mid-sized companies, local service businesses and e-commerce brands that didn't have the budget for a big agency but needed a website that could actually compete. That's been Ocean Development's core focus from the start: closing the gap between what local businesses have online and what their customers actually expect.

Why This Matters

Age isn't the headline here, results are. The point of sharing this isn't to make a case for being young, it's to show what a small, focused team can produce when the work is judged by outcomes instead of credentials.

A website should be measured by what it generates for the business behind it, not how it looks in a portfolio.

What's Next

Ocean Development continues to take on new client work, and Jaden continues to document the process, the wins and the lessons, through byjaden and The Brief. The $3,000,000 milestone is a marker, not a ceiling.
